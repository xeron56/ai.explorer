A diffusion model learns to *reverse* a noise-injection process. We start with a
real sample $x_0$ and gradually add Gaussian noise over $T$ steps:

$$
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\!\left(x_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\; \beta_t I\right).
$$

After enough steps, $x_T$ is indistinguishable from standard Gaussian noise.
Sampling means running the *reverse* chain $p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)$, which
we parameterize with a neural net.

## The reparameterization trick

A handy identity collapses the forward chain into one shot. Define
$\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t (1 - \beta_s)$. Then

$$
x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon,
\quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).
$$

So we never have to simulate $T$ noise steps during training — we sample $t$
uniformly and jump straight to $x_t$.

## The denoising loss

The clean loss-of-record is "predict the noise":

$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, \epsilon}\!\left[ \| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \|^2 \right].
$$

Equivalent up to a constant to a weighted ELBO, but vastly easier to optimize.

## Score matching, the other side of the coin

If you write $p_t(x) \propto e^{-E_t(x)}$, then the optimal denoiser is related
to the *score* $s_t(x) = \nabla_x \log p_t(x)$:

$$
\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \approx -\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\, s_t(x_t).
$$

This is why "score-based" and "denoising-diffusion" people read each other's
papers without translating.

## Sampling

Plain DDPM sampling steps:

```python
@torch.no_grad()
def sample(model, shape, T, betas):
    alphas = 1 - betas
    abar = alphas.cumprod(0)
    x = torch.randn(shape, device=betas.device)
    for t in reversed(range(T)):
        eps = model(x, torch.full((shape[0],), t, device=x.device))
        mean = (x - betas[t] / (1 - abar[t]).sqrt() * eps) / alphas[t].sqrt()
        if t > 0:
            x = mean + betas[t].sqrt() * torch.randn_like(x)
        else:
            x = mean
    return x
```

Variants like DDIM trade stochasticity for speed, and classifier-free guidance
lets you steer samples without a separate classifier.
