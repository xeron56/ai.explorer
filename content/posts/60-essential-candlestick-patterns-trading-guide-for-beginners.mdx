# 60 Essential Candlestick Patterns: Trading Guide for Beginners

Candlestick charts are popular because one candle can show a full price story in a tiny space.

A beginner often looks at a chart and thinks:

```text
Green candle = good
Red candle = bad
Pattern name = trade signal
```

That is too simple.

A better way to think is:

```text
A candlestick is a battle report.
Open  = where the battle started
High  = how far buyers pushed
Low   = how far sellers pushed
Close = who controlled the end of the battle
```

This article teaches candlesticks from zero. You will learn the candle anatomy, the psychology behind the shapes, 60 common patterns, and how to avoid the biggest beginner mistake: trading a pattern name without context.

> Educational note: This article is for learning. Candlestick patterns are not guaranteed predictions. Always combine charts with risk management, broader research, and position sizing.

---

## The one idea that makes candlesticks easier

Imagine you are watching a football match.

The final score matters, but the story of the match also matters.

A team may win 2–1 after dominating the whole match. Another team may win 2–1 after almost losing and scoring at the last minute.

Both results are 2–1, but the stories are different.

Candlesticks work the same way.

A candle does not only tell you whether price ended higher or lower. It also shows how far price traveled before closing.

```text
Big body      = one side controlled most of the period
Small body    = buyers and sellers were close to balanced
Long wick     = price was rejected from that area
Tiny wick     = price did not reject much
Close near high = buyers ended strong
Close near low  = sellers ended strong
```

---

## 1. Candle anatomy: open, high, low, close

Every candle is made from four prices.

```text
Open  = first traded price during the candle period
High  = highest traded price during the period
Low   = lowest traded price during the period
Close = final traded price during the period
```

If you are looking at a daily chart, one candle shows one day. If you are looking at a 5-minute chart, one candle shows five minutes.

### Bullish candle

A bullish candle closes above its open.

```text
Close > Open
```

Example:

```text
Open  = 100
High  = 112
Low   = 96
Close = 108
```

The candle is bullish because:

```text
108 > 100
```

The body size is:

```text
Body = Close - Open
Body = 108 - 100
Body = 8
```

### Bearish candle

A bearish candle closes below its open.

```text
Close < Open
```

Example:

```text
Open  = 100
High  = 105
Low   = 90
Close = 94
```

The candle is bearish because:

```text
94 < 100
```

The body size is:

```text
Body = Open - Close
Body = 100 - 94
Body = 6
```

### Wicks, also called shadows

The wick shows how far price moved beyond the body.

For any candle:

```text
Upper wick = High - max(Open, Close)
Lower wick = min(Open, Close) - Low
Total range = High - Low
Body size = abs(Close - Open)
```

Real-life example:

```text
Open  = 100
High  = 112
Low   = 96
Close = 108

Body size  = abs(108 - 100) = 8
Upper wick = 112 - max(100,108) = 112 - 108 = 4
Lower wick = min(100,108) - 96 = 100 - 96 = 4
Total range = 112 - 96 = 16
```

The candle went as high as 112 and as low as 96, but finally closed at 108.

That means buyers won the close, but both sides fought during the session.

## 3. The smallest candlestick chart renderer in JavaScript

Below is the simplified idea behind the chart in the interactive lab.

You do not need Chart.js, D3, or Recharts to teach the concept. An SVG candle only needs open, high, low, and close.

```jsx
function Candle({ open, high, low, close, x, scaleY }) {
  const bullish = close >= open
  const color = bullish ? "#16a34a" : "#dc2626"

  const wickTop = scaleY(high)
  const wickBottom = scaleY(low)
  const bodyTop = scaleY(Math.max(open, close))
  const bodyBottom = scaleY(Math.min(open, close))
  const bodyHeight = Math.max(2, bodyBottom - bodyTop)

      <line
        x1={x}
        x2={x}
        y1={wickTop}
        y2={wickBottom}
        stroke={color}
        strokeWidth="3"
      />

        x={x - 8}
        y={bodyTop}
        width="16"
        height={bodyHeight}
        rx="3"
        fill={color}
      />
    </g>
  )
}
```

The important part is not the graphics. The important part is the math:

```text
Body = abs(Close - Open)
Range = High - Low
Upper wick = High - max(Open, Close)
Lower wick = min(Open, Close) - Low
```

When you know this math, patterns become easier to understand.

## 4. The three questions you should ask before using any pattern

Do not start with the pattern name.

Start with these three questions.

### Question 1: Where did the pattern appear?

A Hammer near support after a downtrend is meaningful.

A Hammer in the middle of a sideways mess is weaker.

```text
Strong location:
Downtrend → support → Hammer → confirmation

Weak location:
Random sideways chart → Hammer-looking candle → no clear level
```

### Question 2: What is the candle psychology?

Ask who was trapped.

For example, in a Hammer:

```text
Sellers pushed price down.
Buyers rejected the low.
Price closed near the top.
Late sellers may now be trapped.
```

In a Shooting Star:

```text
Buyers pushed price up.
Sellers rejected the high.
Price closed near the low.
Late buyers may now be trapped.
```

### Question 3: Where are you wrong?

A setup without invalidation is not a trade plan.

Example Hammer setup:

```text
Entry idea: above Hammer high
Invalidation: below Hammer low
Target: next resistance
```

If the Hammer low breaks, the buyer-rejection story failed.

## 7. The 60 candlestick patterns explained in beginner language

Use this section like a field guide. For every pattern, look for four things:

```text
1. What it looks like
2. What the buyer-seller story is
3. What confirms it
4. What invalidates it
```


### 1. Marubozu

**Type:** Single candle  
**Bias:** Bullish or bearish

**What it looks like:** Strong momentum candle; can continue a trend or start a reversal after exhaustion.

**Buyer-seller story:** One side controlled almost the entire session. The body is very large and the wicks are tiny or missing.

**Real-life example:** Imagine a stock falls from 120 to 100 over several days. Many late sellers become confident. Then this pattern appears near support and the next candle closes higher. The lesson is not ‘buy blindly’; the lesson is that selling pressure may be weakening and buyers may be starting to defend a level.

**Confirmation:** Next candle should hold beyond the Marubozu close, ideally with volume or a break of structure.

**Invalidation:** Price closes back inside the Marubozu body and removes the momentum message.

**Beginner warning:** If the pattern appears in the middle of a messy sideways range, reduce confidence. The same candle shape is more useful when it appears at a meaningful level.

### 2. Doji

**Type:** Indecision  
**Bias:** Neutral until confirmed

**What it looks like:** Most useful after a strong advance or decline, not in the middle of random sideways noise.

**Buyer-seller story:** Open and close are almost equal, so neither buyers nor sellers won the candle.

**Real-life example:** Imagine price has moved fast and then prints this pattern. The message is uncertainty. A beginner should mark the high and low of the pattern, then wait for price to break one side with conviction.

**Confirmation:** Wait for price to break above or below the Doji range.

**Invalidation:** No follow-through; price keeps chopping around the same range.

**Beginner warning:** If the pattern appears in the middle of a messy sideways range, reduce confidence. The same candle shape is more useful when it appears at a meaningful level.

## 8. How to trade candlesticks without fooling yourself

Candlestick patterns are useful, but they are also easy to misuse.

A beginner usually makes this mistake:

```text
I saw a Hammer.
Therefore price must go up.
```

A professional-style thought process is different:

```text
Price is in a downtrend.
Price reached support.
A Hammer appeared.
The next candle closed above the Hammer high.
My entry, stop, and target create at least 2R reward/risk.
If price breaks the Hammer low, my idea is wrong.
```

The pattern is only one part of the decision.

---

## 9. Confirmation: the candle after the candle

Many candlestick patterns need confirmation.

Confirmation means price does something after the pattern that supports the idea.

Example: Hammer

```text
Pattern: Hammer near support
Confirmation: next candle closes above Hammer high
Invalidation: price closes below Hammer low
```

Example: Shooting Star

```text
Pattern: Shooting Star near resistance
Confirmation: next candle closes below Shooting Star low
Invalidation: price closes above Shooting Star high
```

Example: Doji

```text
Pattern: Doji after big move
Confirmation: price breaks the Doji range
Invalidation: no breakout, price remains choppy
```

Think of confirmation as asking:

```text
Did the market agree with the pattern story?
```

---

## 10. Support and resistance make patterns stronger

A candle pattern becomes more meaningful when it appears at a place where traders already care.

### Support

Support is a price area where buyers have previously stepped in.

Example:

```text
Stock falls to 100 three times.
Each time it bounces.
100 becomes a support area.
```

If a Hammer appears at 100, it matters more than a Hammer in the middle of nowhere.

### Resistance

Resistance is a price area where sellers have previously stepped in.

Example:

```text
Stock rises to 150 three times.
Each time it falls.
150 becomes a resistance area.
```

If a Shooting Star appears at 150, it matters more.

---

## 11. Volume: did many people participate?

Volume shows how much trading happened.

A reversal pattern with high volume can be more interesting because it means many participants were involved.

Example:

```text
Average volume = 1,000,000 shares
Bullish Engulfing volume = 2,500,000 shares
```

This says the reversal was not quiet. Many traders participated.

But volume still does not guarantee success. It only adds evidence.

## 12. Timeframe: the same pattern can mean different things

A pattern on a 5-minute chart may matter for minutes or hours.

A pattern on a weekly chart may matter for weeks or months.

```text
5-minute Hammer  = short-term clue
Daily Hammer     = stronger clue
Weekly Hammer    = bigger market structure clue
```

A beginner should not mix timeframes randomly.

Bad example:

```text
I saw a bullish 5-minute candle, so I will hold for 3 months.
```

Better example:

```text
I use the daily chart for trend.
I use the 1-hour chart for entry.
I use the stop level from the setup.
```

## 13. Risk/reward: a good pattern can still be a bad trade

Suppose you find a beautiful Bullish Engulfing pattern.

Your plan:

```text
Entry = 100
Stop = 90
Target = 105
```

Risk per share:

```text
Risk = Entry - Stop
Risk = 100 - 90
Risk = 10
```

Reward per share:

```text
Reward = Target - Entry
Reward = 105 - 100
Reward = 5
```

Reward/risk:

```text
Reward/risk = Reward ÷ Risk
Reward/risk = 5 ÷ 10
Reward/risk = 0.5R
```

That is weak. You are risking 10 to make 5.

Now compare:

```text
Entry = 100
Stop = 96
Target = 110
```

```text
Risk = 100 - 96 = 4
Reward = 110 - 100 = 10
Reward/risk = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5R
```

This is better because you are risking 1 unit to aim for 2.5 units.

## 14. A simple candlestick scanning logic

A beginner can use simple rules to scan candles, but these rules are not perfect. Different traders use different thresholds.

```js
function candleParts(candle) {
  const { open, high, low, close } = candle
  const body = Math.abs(close - open)
  const range = high - low
  const upperWick = high - Math.max(open, close)
  const lowerWick = Math.min(open, close) - low

  return { body, range, upperWick, lowerWick }
}

function isDoji(candle) {
  const { body, range } = candleParts(candle)
  return range > 0 && body / range <= 0.1
}

function isHammer(candle) {
  const { body, range, upperWick, lowerWick } = candleParts(candle)
  return (
    range > 0 &&
    lowerWick >= body * 2 &&
    upperWick <= body * 0.5 &&
    body / range <= 0.35
  )
}

function isBullishEngulfing(previous, current) {
  const previousBearish = previous.close < previous.open
  const currentBullish = current.close > current.open

  const currentBodyEngulfsPreviousBody =
    current.open < previous.close &&
    current.close > previous.open

  return previousBearish && currentBullish && currentBodyEngulfsPreviousBody
}
```

Important: this code only detects shape. It does not know trend, support, resistance, news, liquidity, or risk/reward.

So a scanner can say:

```text
This looks like a Hammer.
```

But your brain must ask:

```text
Is this Hammer in a useful location?
Is there confirmation?
Where is my stop?
Is the reward worth the risk?
```

## 16. Common beginner mistakes

### Mistake 1: memorizing names without understanding psychology

The name is less important than the story.

```text
Hammer = rejection of lower prices
Shooting Star = rejection of higher prices
Doji = indecision
Engulfing = one side overwhelms the previous candle
```

### Mistake 2: ignoring trend

A bullish pattern against a powerful downtrend is weaker unless it appears at major support and confirms.

### Mistake 3: entering before confirmation

Many patterns fail. Confirmation reduces false signals, although it can also make entry later.

### Mistake 4: using no stop loss

Without invalidation, you are guessing.

### Mistake 5: thinking rare patterns are automatically powerful

Rare names sound exciting, but common clean patterns at good locations are often easier for beginners.

## 18. Final mental model

Candlestick patterns are not magic.

They are a language for reading price behavior.

A beginner should translate every pattern into a simple sentence.

```text
Doji:
The market is undecided.

Hammer:
Sellers pushed down, but buyers rejected the low.

Shooting Star:
Buyers pushed up, but sellers rejected the high.

Bullish Engulfing:
Buyers overwhelmed the previous selling candle.

Bearish Engulfing:
Sellers overwhelmed the previous buying candle.

Rising Three:
The uptrend rested, then continued.

Falling Three:
The downtrend rested, then continued.
```

The strongest beginner formula is:

```text
Good candlestick setup = pattern + location + confirmation + risk plan
```

If one part is missing, be careful.

```text
Pattern only = weak
Pattern + support/resistance = better
Pattern + support/resistance + confirmation = stronger
Pattern + support/resistance + confirmation + good reward/risk = trade plan
```

That is how you use candlestick patterns like a trader, not like a gambler.
