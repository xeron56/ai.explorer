Self-supervised learning (SSL) creates labels *from the data itself* — masked
patches, predicted next tokens, contrasted views. The bet is that solving these
pretext tasks forces the model to discover features that transfer to whatever
you actually care about.

## Contrastive learning, in one equation

The InfoNCE loss says: pull positive pairs together, push everything else apart.

$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{NCE} = -\mathbb{E}\!\left[ \log \frac{\exp(\langle z_i, z_i^+ \rangle / \tau)}{\sum_{j} \exp(\langle z_i, z_j \rangle / \tau)} \right]
$$

with temperature $\tau$ controlling how sharply the model distinguishes the
positive from the negatives.

## Why does it transfer?

The shortest honest answer: under mild conditions, InfoNCE is a lower bound on
the mutual information between two views, $I(Z_1; Z_2)$. Maximizing the bound
maximizes information shared between augmentations, and that shared information
is exactly the "object-level" signal — what stays invariant when you crop,
color-jitter, or mask.

## Non-contrastive methods

BYOL, SimSiam, DINO, and friends remove the negatives entirely. They use one
of three tricks to prevent collapse:

1. A **momentum target** — the target network is an EMA of the online network.
2. A **predictor head** — only the online branch is trained, breaking the
   symmetry that would otherwise let both branches map to a constant.
3. **Centering / sharpening** — DINO uses a moving-average center and a
   temperature mismatch between teacher and student.

```python
# SimSiam-style forward pass
z1, z2 = f(view1), f(view2)
p1, p2 = h(z1), h(z2)
loss = - (cos_sim(p1, z2.detach()) + cos_sim(p2, z1.detach())) / 2
```

The `.detach()` is doing most of the work — it stops the model from co-adapting
its own targets, which is what would otherwise produce constant features.

## What's left to figure out

Why exactly non-contrastive methods *don't* collapse is still not fully settled.
Empirically they work. Theoretically, the explanations are improving but not
yet airtight.
