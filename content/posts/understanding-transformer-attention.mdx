Attention is, at its core, a soft lookup table. Given a query $q$, we want to
fetch a weighted sum of values $V$, where the weights come from how well each
key in $K$ matches the query.

## Scaled dot-product attention

The canonical form is:

$$
\operatorname{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V
$$

where $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k}$, $K \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_k}$, and
$V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_v}$. The $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling keeps the variance
of $QK^\top$ at roughly $1$ before the softmax saturates.

### Why the temperature matters

If $q$ and $k_i$ are independent with unit variance, then
$\mathbb{E}[q \cdot k_i] = 0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(q \cdot k_i) = d_k$.
Without the scaling, large $d_k$ pushes the softmax into a one-hot regime, which
kills gradient flow.

## Multi-head attention

We split the model dimension into $h$ heads:

$$
\operatorname{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h) W^O
$$

with $\text{head}_i = \operatorname{Attention}(Q W_i^Q, K W_i^K, V W_i^V)$.
Each head learns a different relational projection — syntactic structure,
co-reference, positional patterns — and the concatenation gives the next layer a
richer feature mix.

## A small worked example

Suppose $n = m = 4$, $d_k = 2$, and

$$
Q = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix},
\quad K = Q, \quad V = I_4.
$$

Then $\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{2}}$ has its largest entries on the diagonal — each
token attends mostly to itself, with side-lobes wherever query and key align.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def attention(q, k, v):
    scores = q @ k.transpose(-2, -1) / (q.size(-1) ** 0.5)
    return F.softmax(scores, dim=-1) @ v
```

## What attention is *not*

It is not a "memory" — there is no state that persists across forward passes.
It is also not, strictly, a probability distribution over tokens; the softmax
output is a vector in the simplex but the *interpretation* as probability is a
convenient fiction that breaks once you start mixing heads.
