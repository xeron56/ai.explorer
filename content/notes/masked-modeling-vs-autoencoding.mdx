---
title: "Masked Modeling vs. Autoencoding"
description: "Comparing two popular self-supervised objectives."
date: "2024-05-03"
category: "Deep Learning"
---

Both *masked modeling* (BERT, MAE) and classical *autoencoding* reconstruct the
input. But the masking changes what the network is forced to learn.

Autoencoder loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{AE} = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\| x - g(f(x)) \|^2\right]
$$

without a bottleneck, $f \circ g$ can degenerate to the identity. The model
learns *nothing* useful.

Masked modeling loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{MM} = \mathbb{E}_{x, M}\!\left[\| x_M - g(f(x_{\setminus M})) \|^2\right]
$$

The mask $M$ removes information that the encoder *cannot copy through*, so the
network has to model the data distribution to fill it in. This is a much
stronger signal — and a much better pre-training objective.
