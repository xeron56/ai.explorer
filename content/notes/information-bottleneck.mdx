The information bottleneck (IB) principle frames representation learning as
choosing a stochastic mapping $X \to Z$ that compresses $X$ while keeping the
information about $Y$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{IB} = I(Z; Y) - \beta\, I(X; Z).
$$

- $I(Z; Y)$ — how *useful* $Z$ is for predicting $Y$.
- $I(X; Z)$ — how *much of $X$* the representation carries.
- $\beta$ — the trade-off knob.

In deep networks, IB is more lens than literal objective: most architectures
don't explicitly optimize $I(X; Z)$, but you can read training dynamics through
this trade-off and the bend in $(I(X;Z), I(Z;Y))$ curves is often striking.
