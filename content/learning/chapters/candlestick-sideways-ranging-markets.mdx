Not every chart is trending. Many charts spend long periods moving sideways. In the screenshots, this is shown as a cluster of red and green candles boxed inside a horizontal area.

A sideways market means neither buyers nor sellers have full control. Price may still move up and down, but it is not producing clean higher highs or lower lows.

## What a range looks like

A range has two visible boundaries: a top where price repeatedly stalls, and a bottom where price repeatedly bounces.

Inside those boundaries, candles often alternate between red and green. Bodies may be smaller. Wicks may appear on both sides. This is why range movement often feels choppy.

## Why mixed candles matter

When strong buyers are in control, you usually see cleaner green continuation, shallow pullbacks, and higher closes. When strong sellers are in control, you usually see cleaner red continuation, weak bounces, and lower closes.

In a range, neither side can keep control for long. Buyers win one candle, sellers win the next, and price returns toward the middle.

## Price-action psychology behind ranging markets

A ranging market means both sides are active, but neither side has enough conviction to create continuation.

At the range top, buyers become less willing to chase higher prices and sellers become more willing to defend. At the range bottom, sellers become less willing to sell lower and buyers become more willing to step in.

This is why range charts often look frustrating. A bullish candle near the middle can fail because it runs into the top boundary. A bearish candle near the middle can fail because it runs into the bottom boundary. The market is rotating between areas instead of trending cleanly.

## The middle is usually noise

The middle of a range is where signals are weakest. Price has room to move in either direction, and small candles can be misleading.

The edges are more informative. At the top, you can watch whether buyers break through or sellers reject price. At the bottom, you can watch whether sellers break down or buyers defend the zone.

## How a range ends

A range ends when price breaks through a boundary and holds outside it.

The break itself is not enough. A candle can spike above resistance or below support and then fall back inside. A stronger sign is a close outside the range followed by continuation or a successful retest.

## False breakout warnings

Many screenshots show why you should not trust every wick through a level. A wick above the range top means price traded there, but it does not prove buyers accepted that higher area. A wick below the range bottom means price traded lower, but it does not prove sellers gained control.

A stronger breakout has three parts:

- A clear close outside the boundary.
- Follow-through candles that do not immediately return inside the range.
- A retest where the old boundary holds from the other side.

If price breaks out and immediately returns into the range, treat it as a failed breakout until the chart proves otherwise.

## Range reading checklist

Ask these questions:

- Is price making clear higher highs and higher lows?
- Is price making clear lower lows and lower highs?
- Are candles mixed and overlapping?
- Are reactions happening around the same top and bottom?
- Did price close outside the range or only wick through it?

If the answers show overlap and repeated boundary reactions, treat the chart as range-bound until price proves otherwise.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not force a trend label onto a sideways chart. If structure is unclear, the correct answer is often consolidation.

Learning to say "this market is choppy" is a real trading skill. It keeps you from using trend-following logic in a market that is not trending.
