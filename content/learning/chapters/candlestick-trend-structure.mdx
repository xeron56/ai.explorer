Trend direction is not defined by one green candle or one red candle. It is defined by structure.

The screenshots show this clearly: in an uptrend, price keeps making higher highs and higher lows. In a downtrend, price keeps making lower lows and lower highs.

## What a higher high means

A higher high forms when a rally pushes above the previous swing high. This tells you buyers were strong enough to move price into new territory.

One higher high alone is not a complete trend. It is a clue. The trend becomes clearer when higher highs repeat and pullbacks also hold above previous lows.

## What a higher low means

A higher low forms when price pulls back but stops above the prior swing low.

This is important because it shows buyers are willing to step in sooner than before. The market does not need to fall as far to attract demand.

## How uptrends form

An uptrend is built from repeating waves:

- Price rallies and makes a higher high.
- Price pulls back.
- The pullback holds above the prior low.
- Price rallies again and makes another higher high.

This creates an upward stair-step structure. The candles inside each step can be messy, but the swing points tell you the main direction.

## How downtrends form

A downtrend is the opposite:

- Price drops and makes a lower low.
- Price bounces.
- The bounce fails below the prior high.
- Price drops again and makes another lower low.

In a downtrend, green candles can appear, but they are often only temporary bounces until structure changes.

## Break of structure

A break of structure happens when price violates the swing pattern.

In an uptrend, the warning sign is a lower low: price breaks below the prior higher low. In a downtrend, the warning sign is a higher high: price breaks above the prior lower high.

Do not assume every break becomes a reversal. Sometimes it becomes a range. Sometimes it becomes a fakeout. The key point is that the old trend structure is no longer clean.

## How to identify a trend change from uptrend to downtrend

The screenshots show a common sequence:

- Price is in an uptrend and keeps making higher highs and higher lows.
- The next rally fails to make a clean higher high.
- Price then breaks below the prior higher low.
- A bounce forms a lower high instead of recovering the old structure.
- Sellers push price into a lower low.

That is the practical transition from bullish structure to bearish structure. The key is not one red candle. The key is the failure to defend the higher-low pattern.

When you see the first lower low after an uptrend, do not immediately assume a perfect downtrend has started. Treat it as a warning that the prior uptrend is damaged. The next bounce is important. If that bounce cannot reclaim the prior high and instead forms a lower high, the bearish case becomes stronger.

## How to identify a trend change from downtrend to uptrend

The reverse sequence appears in the screenshots too:

- Price is making lower lows and lower highs.
- Sellers push down, but the next low fails to break meaningfully lower.
- Buyers create a higher low.
- Price breaks above a prior lower high.
- A pullback holds above the old low and buyers continue upward.

This is why trend changes are a process. A single green candle after a selloff is only a bounce. A higher low plus a break above a lower high is stronger evidence that control is shifting.

## Price-action psychology behind trends

An uptrend means buyers are becoming more aggressive over time. They are willing to buy at higher prices, and sellers are not strong enough to push price back to old lows. That creates higher lows.

A downtrend means sellers are becoming more aggressive over time. They are willing to sell at lower prices, and buyers are not strong enough to reclaim old highs. That creates lower highs.

This psychology is the reason swing points matter. Higher highs and higher lows are not just chart labels; they show which side is accepting worse prices because they feel urgency.

## Structure checklist

Before labeling a trend, ask:

- What was the last meaningful swing high?
- What was the last meaningful swing low?
- Did price break the prior swing or reject it?
- Was the break followed by continuation or immediate failure?
- Are pullbacks holding structure, or are they breaking it?

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not label a chart as bullish just because the latest candle is green. A green candle inside a lower-high sequence may still be part of a downtrend.

Read the swing structure first. Then use individual candles to judge strength, rejection, and momentum inside that structure.
