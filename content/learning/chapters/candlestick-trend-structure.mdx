Trend direction is not defined by one green candle or one red candle. It is defined by structure.

The screenshots show this clearly: in an uptrend, price keeps making higher highs and higher lows. The pattern is not a straight line upward — it moves in a stair-step rhythm of impulse waves and corrective waves.

## The two moves inside every uptrend

Every uptrend is built from two types of move that alternate repeatedly:

**Run / Impulse move:** This is the strong movement in the direction of the trend — upward. Impulse moves tend to have larger candle bodies, cleaner green closes, and less overlap between consecutive candles. The dominant side (buyers) is in full control during the impulse.

**Pullback / Corrective move:** This is the temporary move against the trend — a dip downward. Corrective moves tend to have smaller bodies, more red and green mixing, and slower price progression. Buyers are still present but sellers have temporarily taken some ground back.

The key distinction: impulse moves cover more distance and move faster. Corrective moves cover less distance and move slower. If corrections become as large or as fast as the impulse moves, the uptrend is starting to weaken.

## What a higher high means

A higher high forms when an impulse move pushes above the previous swing high.

This tells you buyers were strong enough to move price into new territory. Each time a higher high forms, it confirms buyers are still expanding their control.

One higher high alone is not a complete trend. It is a clue. The trend becomes clearer when higher highs repeat and pullbacks also maintain structure.

## What a higher low means

A higher low forms when a corrective move pulls back but stops above the prior swing low.

This is the most important confirmation in uptrend reading. It shows buyers are willing to step in at higher prices than before. The market does not need to fall as far to attract demand.

A higher low also tells you sellers do not have enough control to push price back to the previous floor. Each time a higher low holds, it is evidence the uptrend is healthy.

## How uptrends form step by step

A textbook uptrend builds like this:

1. Price rallies and makes a higher high — the first impulse move.
2. Price pulls back — the first corrective move.
3. The pullback holds above the prior swing low — a higher low is confirmed.
4. Price rallies again and clears the prior high — another higher high.
5. Another pullback forms, holds above the last higher low.
6. The sequence repeats.

This creates an upward stair-step structure. The candles inside each step can be messy, but the swing points tell you the main direction.

## Price action psychology behind uptrends

An uptrend means buyers are becoming more aggressive over time. They are willing to buy at higher prices, and sellers are not strong enough to push price back to old lows. That creates higher lows.

A moving uptrend shows bullish momentum and that buyers are in control. The psychology is often described as an "upwards herd momentum" — buyers are chasing price higher because they expect it to continue rising, and that expectation itself creates more buying.

The practical implication: in an uptrend, you want to trade with the trend and not against it. Fighting an uptrend by expecting it to collapse at every pullback usually means missing the majority of the move.

## Distinguishing the impulse from the correction

The screenshots label these moves explicitly as "Run / Impulse Move" and "Pullback / Corrective Move."

In a healthy uptrend:
- The impulse moves are predominantly green, large-bodied, and cover significant distance quickly.
- The pullback moves are smaller in scale, often mixed red and green, and cover less distance at a slower pace.

When you see these characteristics on a chart, the market is confirming the dominant direction clearly. It is telling you buyers are in control of the impulse and the correction is just temporary relief for sellers.

## Structure checklist

Before labeling a trend as an uptrend, verify:

- What was the last meaningful swing high?
- What was the last meaningful swing low?
- Did the last swing high break above the prior one?
- Did the last pullback hold above the prior low?
- Are the impulse moves notably larger and faster than the corrections?

If the last pullback broke below the prior swing low, that is a structural warning. If the last impulse failed to make a new high, that is a second warning. Two structural warnings in a row suggest the uptrend may be transitioning.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not label a chart as bullish just because the latest candle is green. A green candle inside a lower-high sequence may still be part of a downtrend's corrective bounce.

Read the swing structure first. Then use individual candles to judge strength, rejection, and momentum inside that structure. Candles without structure context are like words without sentences — isolated and hard to interpret correctly.
