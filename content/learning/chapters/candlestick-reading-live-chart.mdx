Reading a candle in theory is straightforward. Reading one on an actual chart with real numbers requires one extra step: knowing where to look for each data point.

The tutorial screenshots use the Apple Inc (`AAPL`) daily chart on NASDAQ from December 2022 to January 2023. Two candles come up repeatedly: January 3 and January 6, 2023. Both candles are used as concrete examples for every OHLC concept that follows.

## How to read a candle on a live chart

On most charting platforms, hover your cursor over any candle. A data panel usually appears showing four numbers labeled O, H, L, C — or Open, High, Low, Close. Some platforms also show a fifth number: V for volume.

The price axis on the right side of the chart is a second way to read values. A horizontal dashed line extends from a specific candle to the right edge, where a number appears in a box. That number shows you the exact price level at that horizontal position.

The screenshots label this specifically: when the cursor hovers over a candle, the dotted line runs to the right edge and the box displays the exact opening, closing, high, or low price of that candle.

## January 3, 2023 — the red candle

The tutorial screenshots walk through the January 3 candle in detail because it captures a bearish session close to the bottom of Apple's decline.

| Data point | Value |
|------------|-------|
| Open       | $130.24 |
| High       | $130.93 |
| Low        | $124.21 |
| Close      | $125.13 |

**Reading open:** When the market opened on January 3, the first trade for Apple stock happened at $130.24. This is the horizontal notch on the left side of the candle body.

**Reading close:** When the market closed on January 3, the last trade happened at $125.13. This is the horizontal notch on the right side of the candle body. Because $125.13 is below $130.24, the candle is red — sellers won this day.

**Reading the high:** The wick extends above the body all the way to $130.93. That is the highest price anyone paid for Apple on that day. Buyers briefly pushed above $130 but could not sustain it.

**Reading the low:** The wick extends below the body all the way to $124.21. That is the lowest price any seller accepted. From $124.21, buyers stepped back in and pushed price up to close at $125.13.

Why this day is bearish: price opened at $130.24 and finished at $125.13 — a decline of $5.11 in a single session. That is a meaningful move, and it continued the downward pressure Apple had been experiencing since mid-December.

## January 6, 2023 — the green candle

The very next trading day shows the opposite result. January 6 was a bullish session.

| Data point | Value |
|------------|-------|
| Open       | $126.05 |
| High       | $130.30 |
| Low        | $124.90 |
| Close      | $129.61 |

**Open:** Apple opened at $126.05 — below the prior day's close of $125.13? No. Friday January 6 actually gapped open slightly higher than Thursday's close, reflecting overnight order flow.

**Close:** Apple finished the day at $129.61. Because $129.61 is above the open of $126.05, this candle is green. Buyers controlled the entire session.

**High:** The upper wick extends to $130.30 — buyers pushed all the way back toward the January 3 open before sellers created some resistance.

**Low:** The lower wick extends to $124.90 — just below the prior day's low of $124.21. This shows sellers tested lower levels early in the session but buyers immediately rejected that area and price recovered.

Why this day is bullish: price appreciated $3.56 in one session after the prior selloff. Buyers stepped in near the recent low and drove price almost 5% higher by the close.

## What the two candles together tell you

January 3 was a strong bearish day. January 6 immediately followed with a strong bullish day. Taken together, these two candles describe a moment where sellers pushed hard, hit a floor, and then buyers responded.

This is the kind of pattern that produces support areas and potential reversals. Neither candle alone tells the full story — context requires reading both.

## How to use the price axis

The vertical scale on the right side of the chart shows the price range visible in the current window. You can read the approximate price of any candle by tracing a horizontal line across to the right axis.

For precise readings, hover over the candle. The dashed horizontal line that appears crosses the right axis at an exact price in a highlighted box. The screenshots show this for every OHLC value — the dotted line runs from the candle's open, close, high, or low all the way to the right-edge box.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not try to memorize exact price numbers. The lesson from the January 3 and January 6 candles is not the specific dollar values. The lesson is the reading method:

1. Is close above or below open? (Color tells you the winner.)
2. How big is the body? (Distance between open and close tells you the strength.)
3. How long are the wicks? (Wick length tells you where price explored but rejected.)
4. What is the context from the candles around it? (One candle is a clue; a sequence is a story.)

Apply that method to every candle on any chart, at any price level.
