A candlestick is not just a red or green block. It is a compact summary of what happened during one chosen time period: where price opened, how far it moved up, how far it moved down, and where it closed.

If the chart is on a `1D` setting, each candle represents one full trading day. If the chart is on `1H`, each candle represents one hour. The same reading process works on every time frame — only the amount of time represented changes.

## Why read candlesticks at all

Candlesticks tell a story about what large institutions — the buyers and sellers who move markets — are doing at any moment in time. The pattern that forms at a particular moment reflects real buying and selling pressure, not random movement.

Traders use candlestick patterns and their movement to make trading decisions. This approach is called price action reading. The raw information inside each candle — open, high, low, close — is the foundation of price action.

## Green candles versus red candles

**Green candle:** Close is above open. Buyers controlled the period. Price appreciated and "went upwards." The body runs from the open (bottom) to the close (top).

**Red candle:** Close is below open. Sellers controlled the period. Price depreciated and "went downwards." The body runs from the open (top) to the close (bottom).

The color is the first thing to read because it tells you immediately who won the period.

## Body size tells you momentum strength

This is one of the most practical readings in candlestick analysis:

**Large body = greater momentum.** When the body is long, price traveled a significant distance during the period. A large green body shows buyers pushed aggressively. A large red body shows sellers pushed aggressively. The bigger the body, the stronger the conviction behind the move.

**Small body = weaker momentum.** When the body is short, price barely moved from open to close even if wicks extended in both directions. A small body shows hesitation, indecision, or a balance between buyers and sellers.

Body size is most meaningful in context. A large body after a period of small bodies signals an acceleration. A large body inside an established trend is continuation. A large body appearing after an extended move in the opposite direction is a warning worth watching.

## What the body tells you

The candle body is the thick section between the open and close.

A green candle means price closed above where it opened. Buyers controlled enough of the period to finish higher.

A red candle means price closed below where it opened. Sellers controlled enough of the period to finish lower.

Pay attention to where within the overall range the body closes. A candle that closes near its high shows buyers dominated right through to the end of the period. A candle that closes near its low shows sellers dominated. A candle that closes in the middle had a more contested session.

## What the wick tells you

The wick, also called the shadow or tail, shows price movement that did not hold into the close.

An upper wick means price traded higher, but sellers pushed it back down before the period ended. A lower wick means price traded lower, but buyers pushed it back up before the period ended.

Do not read a wick in isolation. A long upper wick after a long rally has a different meaning from a long upper wick inside a sideways range. Context decides whether the wick is important.

Long wicks at significant price levels — support, resistance, or major swing points — carry more weight than the same wick in the middle of a trend with no nearby structure.

## Why doji candles show indecision

A doji forms when the open and close are almost the same, producing a very small or invisible body with wicks extending in both directions.

Buyers may have pushed price up, sellers may have pushed price down, but neither side finished with a clean win. Price swung up and swung down, but then closed close to the same price it opened at — meaning buyers and sellers got nowhere.

That is why a doji is a sign of indecision, not an automatic reversal signal. It says momentum paused or balance appeared. The next candles decide whether that pause becomes continuation, consolidation, or reversal.

A doji after a strong trend has more significance than a doji in the middle of a choppy range. In an established trend, it signals the dominant side may be losing conviction.

## Read OHLC before reading patterns

Most beginners jump directly to pattern names: hammer, engulfing, morning star, doji. That is backwards. First read the raw information:

- Where did the candle open?
- Where was the highest price?
- Where was the lowest price?
- Where did the candle close?
- Did the body finish near the high, the low, or the middle?

## A simple candle reading routine

**Start with time frame.** A candle on a daily chart carries different decision weight than a candle on a five-minute chart.

**Compare open and close.** This tells you which side won the period and how strongly (body size = strength).

**Compare the wicks.** This tells you where price tried to go but failed to stay.

**Assess body size relative to recent candles.** Is this candle larger or smaller than the ones before it? Growing candles show increasing momentum. Shrinking candles show fading momentum.

**Compare the candle to nearby candles.** One candle gives a clue; a group of candles gives structure.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not treat one candle as a prediction. A candle is evidence. Evidence becomes useful when it lines up with trend, support, resistance, volume, market context, and risk management.

The goal is not to memorize every pattern name. The goal is to understand what price attempted, what price rejected, and who controlled the close.
