A candlestick is not just a red or green block. It is a compact summary of what happened during one chosen time period: where price opened, how far it moved up, how far it moved down, and where it closed.

If the screenshot is on a `1D` chart, each candle represents one full trading day. If the chart is on `1H`, each candle represents one hour. The same reading process works on every time frame; only the amount of time represented changes.

## What the body tells you

The candle body is the thick section between the open and close.

A green candle means price closed above where it opened. Buyers controlled enough of the period to finish higher.

A red candle means price closed below where it opened. Sellers controlled enough of the period to finish lower.

Body size matters. A large body means one side moved price a meaningful distance. A tiny body means the market opened and closed near the same level, even if price moved around during the session.

## What the wick tells you

The wick, also called the shadow, shows price movement that did not hold into the close.

An upper wick means price traded higher, but sellers pushed it back down before the period ended. A lower wick means price traded lower, but buyers pushed it back up before the period ended.

Do not read a wick in isolation. A long upper wick after a long rally has a different meaning from a long upper wick inside a sideways range. Context decides whether the wick is important.

## Why doji candles show indecision

A doji forms when the open and close are almost the same. Buyers may have pushed price up, sellers may have pushed price down, but neither side finished with a clean win.

That is why a doji is a sign of indecision, not an automatic reversal signal. It says momentum paused or balance appeared. The next candles decide whether that pause becomes continuation, consolidation, or reversal.

## Read OHLC before reading patterns

Most beginners jump directly to names like hammer, engulfing, morning star, or doji. That is backwards. First read the raw information:

- Where did the candle open?
- Where was the highest price?
- Where was the lowest price?
- Where did the candle close?
- Did the body finish near the high, the low, or the middle?

## A simple candle reading routine

Start with time frame. A candle on a daily chart carries a different decision weight than a candle on a five-minute chart.

Then compare open and close. This tells you which side won the period.

Next compare the wicks. This tells you where price tried to go but failed to stay.

Finally compare the candle to nearby candles. One candle gives a clue; a group of candles gives structure.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not treat one candle as a prediction. A candle is evidence. Evidence becomes useful when it lines up with trend, support, resistance, volume, market context, and risk management.

The goal is not to memorize every pattern. The goal is to understand what price attempted, what price rejected, and who controlled the close.
