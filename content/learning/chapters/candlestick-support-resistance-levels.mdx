Support and resistance are the first chart structures to learn after candle anatomy. They explain why price often reacts around the same area more than once.

In the screenshots, the Apple chart shows price falling, pausing around a lower level, and later moving back above that area. That is the kind of repeated reaction that makes a level worth marking.

## What support means

Support is an area where buying pressure has appeared before. It does not guarantee that price will rise. It simply tells you that the market has previously treated that area as attractive enough for buyers to respond.

Candles near support often show lower wicks, mixed red and green bodies, or smaller selling candles. Those signs suggest sellers are still active, but their control may be weakening.

## What resistance means

Resistance is an area where selling pressure has appeared before. It does not guarantee that price will fall. It tells you that buyers previously struggled to push through that zone.

Candles near resistance often show upper wicks, failed breakouts, or strong green candles that quickly lose follow-through.

## Levels are zones, not magic numbers

The screenshot labels exact prices, but real chart reading should think in zones.

A level can include candle bodies, wicks, and nearby closes. If several candles react around `130`, you should not assume `130.24` is the only meaningful price. The useful idea is that market participants responded around that area.

## How to mark a useful level

Look for at least two reactions. One touch is only a point. Two or more reactions start to form a level.

Prefer levels that caused visible behavior change. A level matters more when price rejected it strongly, paused around it, or broke through and later returned to it.

Use bodies and closes for cleaner structure, then check wicks for rejection. Bodies show where the market accepted price. Wicks show where price explored but failed to stay.

## Break and retest

When price breaks above resistance, that former resistance can become support. When price breaks below support, that former support can become resistance.

This is not because the line has power. It happens because traders who acted around that area may change behavior after price proves them wrong or confirms their expectation.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not draw too many levels. If every candle has a line, no level is useful. Mark only the areas that clearly changed price behavior.

A good level should help you answer one question: if price returns here, what reaction would prove buyers or sellers are still active?
