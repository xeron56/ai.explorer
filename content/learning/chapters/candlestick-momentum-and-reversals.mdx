Momentum tells you how aggressively price is moving. Trend tells you direction; momentum tells you force.

Reading momentum helps you understand whether the dominant side is accelerating, holding steady, or beginning to lose control. The screenshots identify five distinct signals split between momentum gain and momentum loss.

## Momentum gain signal 1: tight price movement

Tight movement means candles keep advancing in one direction with minimal overlap and shallow counter-moves. Pullbacks are shallow, and price does not spend much time moving sideways.

In a bullish tight move, each new candle opens close to where the prior candle closed and advances further in the upward direction. There are not enough sellers in the market to cause wide swings in the opposite direction.

In a bearish tight move, the same applies downward. Sellers are in full control of the direction and buyers cannot create wide bounces.

The key observation: tight price movement without wide swings shows the dominant side is in complete control. This is momentum gain.

## Momentum gain signal 2: candles growing in size

When candle bodies get larger with each successive period, each candle is covering more distance. This can show expanding participation and stronger conviction.

In a bullish sequence, bodies growing progressively from small to large means buyers are becoming more aggressive — each new period they push price further from open to close. You want to trade with this upwards gain of momentum, not against it.

In a bearish sequence, the same concept applies downward: each red candle is larger and covers a greater distance per period, showing a gain in bearish momentum.

Do not fight obvious momentum just because price "looks high" or "looks low." Strong trends can remain strong longer than expected when momentum is expanding.

## Momentum loss signal 1: wide swings after tight movement

After tight directional movement, wide swings are one of the earliest signs that momentum is losing.

Wide swings mean larger price oscillations start appearing — candles moving up and down with more overlap and more conflict between buyers and sellers. If the prior move was tight and clean, the appearance of wide swings shows the dominant side is no longer in complete control.

In a bullish tight move that transitions to wide swings: buyers are still present, but sellers are now creating meaningful reactions. Wide swings after tight movement show that momentum loss is occurring and the buyers are no longer in complete control.

If paired with trend change price action (a structural confirmation), wide swings after a tight run can lead to a possible reversal. This is the earliest of the three momentum loss signals.

## Momentum loss signal 2: candles shrinking in size

Shrinking candle bodies are the second momentum loss signal.

When each successive candle covers less distance than the one before it, the dominant side is losing push. Buyers or sellers are still moving price in the same direction, but each period they carry it a shorter distance.

A sequence of candles where the bodies get progressively smaller signals loss of momentum of the trend. The move is not over, but it is weakening. When the bodies shrink to near-doji size at the end of a move, the balance between buyers and sellers is becoming roughly equal — and that is often the period just before a reversal or significant pause.

Candles shrinking in size can trigger a possible reversal and presents trade opportunities in the opposite direction — but only when paired with context (structure, levels, and a confirmation signal).

## Momentum loss signal 3: candle colour change

A colour change is the third momentum loss signal and often the most visible.

After several consecutive green candles, the first meaningful red candle shows buyers failed to produce another higher close and sellers finally produced a lower close. The entire run of same-colour candles shows the dominant side was in full control. The first opposite-colour candle shows that control has been interrupted.

Red candle appearing after all-green up move: price failed to make another candle with a higher closing price, and instead made a red candle with a lower closing price.

Green candle appearing after all-red down move: price failed to make another candle with a lower closing price, and instead made a green candle with a higher closing price.

The colour change is stronger when it appears after an extended move of the same colour, near resistance or support, or after candle bodies have already started shrinking.

## Reversal warning versus reversal confirmation

A warning is not confirmation.

- Wide swings after tight movement warn that the dominant side is no longer in complete control.
- Shrinking candles warn that momentum is fading.
- A colour change warns that the opposite side has appeared.

A structure break confirms that the trend rhythm has changed.

For example, in an uptrend, a red candle near the top is only a warning. A break below the prior higher low is a stronger structural signal. Momentum signals tell you to watch closely; structural signals tell you what happened.

## Using momentum signals together

The most reliable momentum reading combines multiple signals:

**Momentum gain:** Tight movement AND growing candle bodies. When both appear together, the dominant side has full control and is increasing strength.

**Momentum loss:** Wide swings OR shrinking candles followed by a colour change. When these stack together, the dominant side is clearly weakening.

A single signal is less reliable than two or three signals appearing in sequence. The tutorial screenshots show this stacking approach throughout — look for the transition from tight-and-growing to wide-or-shrinking before expecting a meaningful reversal.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not short every first red candle after a rally, and do not buy every first green candle after a selloff.

Use the colour change as a warning sign. Then wait for context: level reaction, momentum shift, and structure change. A single candle colour change in the middle of a strong trend is usually a pullback candle, not a reversal signal.

Momentum signals are most useful when they appear at the end of extended moves, near significant levels, and in combination with structural signals from swing points.
