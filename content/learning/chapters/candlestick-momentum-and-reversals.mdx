Momentum tells you how aggressively price is moving. Trend tells you direction; momentum tells you force.

The screenshots show two sides of momentum reading. First, momentum gain: candles move tightly in one direction and often grow larger. Then momentum loss: candles shrink, overlap increases, and the first strong opposite-color candle appears after an extended move.

## Momentum gain clue 1: tight movement

Tight movement means candles keep moving in one direction with limited overlap. Pullbacks are shallow, and price does not spend much time moving sideways.

In a bullish move, tight movement often means buyers are willing to pay higher prices quickly. In a bearish move, tight movement often means sellers are accepting lower prices quickly.

The key is clean progression. Momentum is stronger when price advances without repeatedly giving back the entire prior candle.

## Momentum gain clue 2: candles growing in size

When candle bodies get larger, each period is covering more distance. This can show expanding participation and stronger conviction.

In the screenshot sequence, the bullish candles grow as price accelerates upward. The lesson is simple: do not fight obvious momentum just because price "looks high." Strong trends can remain strong longer than expected.

## Momentum loss clue 1: candles shrinking in size

Momentum loss often begins before a reversal. You may see smaller bodies, weaker closes, and more overlap near the end of a move.

This does not mean price must reverse immediately. It means the dominant side is no longer expanding control as easily as before.

## Momentum loss clue 2: color change

After several strong green candles, the first meaningful red candle deserves attention. It shows buyers failed to create another higher close and sellers finally produced a lower close.

After several strong red candles, the first meaningful green candle can show the same shift in the opposite direction.

The color change is stronger when it appears after an extended move, near resistance or support, or after candle bodies have already started shrinking.

## Reversal warning versus reversal confirmation

A warning is not confirmation.

Shrinking candles warn that momentum is fading. A color change warns that the opposite side has appeared. A structure break confirms that the trend rhythm has changed.

For example, in an uptrend, a red candle near the top is only a warning. A break below the prior higher low is a stronger structural signal.

## Momentum reading checklist

Ask these questions:

- Are candles moving tightly in one direction?
- Are bodies growing or shrinking?
- Are wicks rejecting continuation?
- Is price still making higher highs or lower lows?
- Did the first opposite-color candle break an important swing level?

Momentum is most useful when combined with structure. Strong momentum inside a trend supports continuation. Momentum loss near a major level warns that the trend may pause, range, or reverse.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not short every first red candle after a rally, and do not buy every first green candle after a selloff.

Use the candle as a warning sign. Then wait for context: level reaction, momentum shift, and structure change.
