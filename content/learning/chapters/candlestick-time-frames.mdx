Before you read a single candle, you need to know what time period that candle is compressing. A candlestick chart has a time frame setting, and that setting determines how much time each bar represents.

The Apple screenshots in this tutorial series are all on the `1D` daily chart. That label in the top-left corner is the most important thing to check before you read anything else.

## What a time frame actually means

A time frame tells the chart software: "combine all the price activity during this time window into one candle."

On a `1D` chart, every candle contains the open, high, low, and close for one complete trading day — from when the market opened in the morning to when it closed in the afternoon.

On a `1H` chart, the same window is chopped into one-hour slices. A single daily candle that took eight hours to form would become approximately eight separate one-hour candles showing the same journey in finer detail.

The market did not change. The story is the same. Only the zoom level changed.

## The time frame selector

On most charting platforms you will see a row of buttons at the top of the chart: `1m`, `5m`, `15m`, `1H`, `4H`, `1D`, `1W`. Each one switches how much time each candle covers.

| Label | One candle covers |
|-------|-------------------|
| `1m`  | 1 minute |
| `5m`  | 5 minutes |
| `15m` | 15 minutes |
| `1H`  | 1 hour |
| `4H`  | 4 hours |
| `1D`  | 1 trading day |
| `1W`  | 1 full week (Monday to Friday) |

The date axis at the bottom of the chart adjusts automatically. On `1D` you see day labels. On `1H` you see hour labels.

## Why the daily chart is the best starting point

Beginners almost always get lost on short time frames first.

A `5m` chart of any large stock or index shows dozens of candles forming every hour, each one reacting to tiny fluctuations in order flow. The signal-to-noise ratio is low. Small random price movements look like patterns.

The `1D` chart compresses those fluctuations into one bar per day. The result is a much cleaner picture of whether buyers or sellers actually won each session. Trends are easier to see, support and resistance levels are more reliable, and pattern recognition is far less prone to false readings.

Start on the daily chart. Learn to read structure, trends, and levels there first. Then, once you have a clear directional view on the daily, you can drop to a lower time frame to look for a more precise entry.

## How the same market looks on different time frames

Imagine Apple stock:
- On `1D`: you see one candle per trading session — roughly 22 candles for a full month of trading.
- On `1H`: the same month becomes roughly 132 candles because the US market has approximately six trading hours per day.
- On `1W`: the same month shrinks to about four candles, each representing a full week.

The overall shape — a downtrend, a recovery, a range — will look similar across all three, but the detail level changes dramatically. A single daily candle with a long lower wick might look like ten small hourly candles, several of which are red and several green, while still producing that one bullish daily close.

## Multi-time-frame context

Professional traders use at least two time frames together:

1. **Higher time frame** — defines the overall direction (daily or weekly).
2. **Lower time frame** — reveals entry-level detail (4H or 1H).

The rule is simple: read the bigger picture first, then use the smaller picture to refine. If the daily chart is in a clear downtrend, a bullish signal on the 15-minute chart should be treated as a counter-trend setup with extra caution.

Never use only one time frame in isolation. The daily chart shows you the trend. The hourly chart shows you the conditions inside that trend. Both pieces of information matter.

## The date axis is your anchor

The horizontal axis at the bottom of every chart tells you exactly when each candle started. On the `1D` chart, you will see date labels like `Jan 3` or `Jan 6`. These are the dates the tutorial screenshots refer to when they point to specific candles.

When a screenshot says "the Apple stock was trading at $126.05 on January 6," that means the daily candle for January 6 opened at that price. The date axis is what ties each candle to a specific real-world event.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not switch between time frames rapidly trying to find a chart that "looks good." Every time frame will show different candle patterns on the same asset at the same moment.

Choose a primary time frame, read the structure there consistently, and only switch to a lower time frame when you have a clear directional view you want to refine. Time frame hopping creates confusion, not clarity.
