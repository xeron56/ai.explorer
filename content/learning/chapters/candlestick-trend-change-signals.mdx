A trend does not flip instantly. It weakens first, then breaks structure, then builds the opposite structure.

The tutorial screenshots show two transition sequences: uptrend changing to downtrend, and downtrend changing to uptrend. Each transition has three recognizable signals. Learning to read them in order tells you whether a trend change is just starting, forming, or confirmed.

## Uptrend changing to a downtrend

An uptrend is in place while price keeps making higher highs and higher lows. The question is what tells you that rhythm is about to break or has already broken.

The screenshots identify three signals, presented in order of how they often appear:

### Signal 1: The first lower low forms

A lower low happens when a pullback breaks below the prior higher low.

In a clean uptrend, pullbacks stop above the previous low. The higher-low sequence is what creates the rising stair-step. When one pullback fails to hold above the prior low, that sequence is broken.

This is the earliest warning. Price may recover and resume the uptrend — false breaks happen. But the first lower low is the moment to stop adding to bullish positions and start watching the next bounce closely.

One lower low does not confirm a downtrend. It is a structural warning that the uptrend is damaged.

### Signal 2: Lower low and lower high both present

If the bounce after the first lower low cannot reclaim the prior swing high, a lower high forms. Now you have both a lower low and a lower high.

This is stronger evidence. The market has now shown:
- Buyers failed to hold the prior low (lower low).
- Buyers failed to recover the prior high (lower high).

The bearish structure is beginning to repeat. One sequence of lower low and lower high is still early, but it is much more significant than the first lower low alone.

### Signal 3: Lower high forms before the lower low

In some transitions, the trend change signal appears at the top rather than the bottom.

The rally that should make a new higher high instead stops below the prior high. This lower high at the top is the earliest possible warning — it shows buyers could not push into new territory even before a lower low has formed.

When a lower high forms first, watch the next pullback. If it makes a lower low, both signals are now in place and the downtrend case is building.

## Downtrend changing to an uptrend

A downtrend is in place while price keeps making lower lows and lower highs. The opposite transition follows the same logic.

### Signal 1: The first higher high forms

A higher high happens when a bounce breaks above the prior lower high.

In a clean downtrend, every rally is capped below the previous peak. The lower-high sequence is what creates the falling stair-step. When a bounce finally pushes above the prior lower high, that sequence is broken.

This is the earliest warning. Sellers may reassert and push the market back down — failed breakouts happen. But the first higher high is the moment to stop adding to bearish positions and start watching the next pullback closely.

### Signal 2: Higher high and higher low both present

If the pullback after the first higher high holds above the prior swing low, a higher low forms. Now you have both a higher high and a higher low.

This combination is the beginning of an uptrend structure:
- Buyers pushed into new high territory (higher high).
- Buyers absorbed selling pressure without breaking to new lows (higher low).

The bullish structure is beginning to repeat. Two data points is still early, but both together are significantly more meaningful than the higher high alone.

### Signal 3: Higher low forms before the higher high

Some transitions signal from the bottom. A drop that should make a new lower low instead holds above the prior swing low. That higher low shows buyers are defending at a higher floor before any new highs have appeared.

When a higher low forms first, watch the next rally. If it makes a higher high, both signals are in place and the uptrend case is building.

## Why a trend change is a process

The screenshots emphasize this point repeatedly: do not expect a clean one-candle reversal.

A typical uptrend-to-downtrend transition looks like this:
1. Rally stalls and fails to make a new high.
2. Pullback breaks below the prior higher low.
3. Bounce forms but fails below the new lower high.
4. Price drops to a new lower low.
5. The sequence has now repeated — bearish structure is establishing.

Each step involves multiple candles, some of which may look briefly bullish. The key is watching the swing points, not individual candles.

## Warning versus confirmation

A warning is the first signal: the first lower low, the first higher high, the first lower high before a lower low.

Confirmation is when the new structure repeats. A downtrend is confirmed by a sequence of lower lows and lower highs, not just one of each. An uptrend reversal is confirmed by a sequence of higher highs and higher lows.

The practical consequence: warnings tell you to pay attention and reduce risk. Confirmations tell you the new direction has enough structure to trade with.

## Common sequence in practice

The typical progression for uptrend to downtrend, as the screenshots show:

- **Step 1:** Price is in a clear uptrend with higher highs and higher lows.
- **Step 2:** Rally produces a smaller gain than usual — or even fails to reach a new high.
- **Step 3:** Pullback breaks below the most recent higher low — first lower low.
- **Step 4:** Bounce forms. This bounce becomes the critical test. If it recovers the prior high, the uptrend may resume. If it fails below, a lower high is confirmed.
- **Step 5:** Price drops to a new lower low. The lower-low, lower-high sequence has now repeated at least once.

At step 4, many traders are confused because green candles appear. Those green candles are the corrective bounce. The question is whether they can break the prior high — if they cannot, the bearish case keeps building.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not try to call a trend change at the first candle that moves in the opposite direction.

One green candle after a downtrend is a potential higher high candidate — it is not confirmation. One red candle after an uptrend is a potential lower low candidate — it is not confirmation.

Wait for the structure to repeat. The first signal is a warning. The second and third are confirmation. Trading the first signal alone has a much higher failure rate than waiting for the structural pattern to establish itself over multiple swings.
