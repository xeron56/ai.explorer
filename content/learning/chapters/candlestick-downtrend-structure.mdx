A downtrend is the exact mirror of an uptrend. Where an uptrend builds higher highs and higher lows, a downtrend builds lower lows and lower highs.

The pattern is the same type of repeating structure — impulse moves in the dominant direction, followed by corrections against it — but the dominant direction is now down, and sellers are in control.

## What a lower low means

A lower low forms when a selling wave drops below the prior swing low.

This tells you sellers were strong enough to push price past the previous floor. Buyers who stepped in at the old low were not enough to absorb the supply coming in from above.

One lower low is a warning. A sequence of lower lows is the definition of a downtrend.

## What a lower high means

A lower high forms when a bounce stops and reverses below the prior swing high.

This is one of the most important signals in downtrend reading. It tells you buyers tried to reclaim higher territory and failed. Sellers defended the market at a lower level than before.

A lower high combined with a lower low is the full confirmation of bearish structure. The market is making lower ceilings and lower floors at the same time.

## Impulse drops versus corrective bounces

A downtrend is not a vertical line straight down. It moves in waves:

**Impulse drop (the run):** The strong movement downward. These are often sequences of red candles with large bodies, minimal overlap, and clear lower closes. The impulse is where sellers have full momentum.

**Corrective bounce (the pullback):** A temporary move upward against the dominant direction. These are usually smaller candles, more red and green mixing, and weaker closes. The bounce shows buyers exist, but not in enough force to reverse the trend.

The critical observation: in a downtrend, the corrective bounces are consistently weaker than the impulse drops. If a bounce suddenly becomes as large as the impulse moves, that is a sign momentum may be shifting.

## How a downtrend forms step by step

The screenshots trace this sequence clearly:

1. Price was moving upward (higher highs and higher lows in place).
2. A selling wave drops price to a new low, breaking the prior higher low.
3. A bounce forms but fails below the prior swing high — the first lower high.
4. Sellers push price to another lower low, now below the first.
5. The bounce again fails below the previous high.
6. The sequence repeats: lower low, lower high, lower low, lower high.

Once this stair-step pattern is established and repeating, the chart is in a downtrend. Green candles still appear during the bounces, but they are part of the correction, not a trend change.

## Price action psychology behind a downtrend

A downtrend tells you sellers are becoming more aggressive over time. They are willing to sell at lower prices than before, which creates lower highs. Buyers are not strong enough to push price back to old levels, which confirms the lower high pattern.

The same traders who bought at prior highs are now sitting on losses. When price bounces, many of them sell to reduce their exposure. That selling is one of the reasons bounces fail — supply appears at progressively lower levels.

This is often called "selling into strength." As price briefly rises during a bounce, sellers use the rally to exit or add to short positions. The result is that each bounce runs into resistance sooner than the previous one, forming the lower high sequence.

## Downtrend versus a pullback in an uptrend

Not every red candle sequence is a downtrend.

In an uptrend, price regularly pulls back for one, two, or three candles before continuing higher. Those red candles are corrective moves inside a larger bullish structure. A pullback in an uptrend does not produce lower highs — the next swing high still clears the previous one after the dip ends.

A true downtrend requires repeated failures to make new highs. The bounces must keep stopping lower and lower. If each bounce is reaching new highs or at least matching prior highs, the downtrend interpretation is premature.

## Downtrend reading checklist

Before labeling a chart as a downtrend, verify:

- Did the most recent swing low break below the prior swing low?
- Did the most recent bounce stop below the prior swing high?
- Has this lower-low, lower-high sequence repeated at least twice?
- Are the impulse moves larger and more decisive than the corrections?
- Has a candle or sequence definitively broken below a prior meaningful support level?

If the answer to most of these is yes, the chart is likely in a downtrend.

## Beginner mistake to avoid

Do not label a chart as a downtrend after one red candle or one drop. A single red candle inside an uptrend is often just a pullback. A spike down followed by an immediate recovery is often a wick.

The downtrend label requires structure: a pattern of lower lows and lower highs that has repeated. The patience to wait for structural confirmation — rather than reacting to one candle — separates reading that is useful from reading that is reactive.
